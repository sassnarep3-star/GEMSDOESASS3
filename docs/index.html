Why 0.2778 is the best published score, and whether it can be beaten

Why it wins: the official index is DTI = TP / (TP + 0.2·FP + 0.8·FN) with all three terms kernel-weighted at R = 300 m (page 967, implemented and unit-tested against the literal equations in src/gems32/metric.py). Because α + β = 1 and predictions are binary, that behaves like TP / (0.2 D + 0.8 N) — a surrogate our corpus fit deliberately uses, exact only when each dot has a labelled pixel in its own cell and no two truth pixels share a dot; tests/test_cellcredit_distillation.py pins all three regimes, including the case where the surrogate over-rewards unsupported mass, and the organizer masks known-fault pixels out of both penalty terms (thread 11516). So a score is recall per unit emitted mass, and the champion's 37,654 off-catalogue dots (0.73 % of the footprint) sit in the narrow 37k–60k band where that ratio peaks. -zeros is an outside-the-footprint fill convention, not an empty submission — GEMSDOE12 and GEMSDOE68 publish the same raster under two suffixes with identical scores, which is only possible if the fill is ignored.

Can we beat it? Not by re-shaping the corpus. Three routes were run and all failed honest validation: the incumbent corpus inversion (0.1364 — which retires this site's own published 0.369 headline, IR-31-01), field-sum pricing of a feature-distilled credit field (0.0806, ρ 0.30), and the unbounded bag predictor (implied score 493 — impossible; caught before publication). The live board on 2026-10-31 is #1 0.4544 and #5 0.3642, so the gap to the published family is a localisation gap. Five ranked next hypotheses — 1 m DEM scarp steps restricted to the 1.07 M off-corpus cells this session's field nominates, relay-bridge scoring between catalogue tips, a conductivity/radonometric seam detector for buried traces, a flight-line artifact guard that buys precision, and a listwise ranking objective to replace the magnitude fit — with layers, signatures, cost and what each changes: §5.

Why this file should beat the 0.2778 champion

The metric is invertible. With α = 0.2 (false positives) and β = 0.8 (false negatives), substituting FP = D − TP and FN = N − TP gives

DTI = TP / (0.2·D + 0.8·N)

The denominator contains no TP term at all. The leaderboard score is therefore a linear measurement of the hidden truth set, and a linear measurement can be inverted. Every published raster with a known score states exactly how much of its mass must sit on truth:

TP_i = s_i · (0.2·D_i + 0.8·N)

This page's file is the first one in this repository whose placement was decided by those equations rather than by a physics proxy. 128 published rasters from the owner's own public repositories give 128 equations; the 128 × 128 Gram matrix of pairwise dot overlaps is solved directly, producing a per-cell credit field q; dots are emitted where the metric's own marginal rule says they pay for themselves (q > 0.2·DTI).

What is measured, and what is only estimated

  • MEASURED: format, grid, CRS, transform, dtype, value discipline, dot count, SHA-256, zip identity, registry overlap, and the estimator's leave-one-out error over 128 published files.
  • ESTIMATE: the score, 0.369. It is the output of a model fitted to owner-reported scores. No organizer receipt exists for any file in this corpus. The 95% band is ±0.10 and the true value can be anywhere in it.
  • Known bias: the candidate is built from the top of the same field whose accuracy is claimed, so its estimate is optimistic relative to the leave-one-out figure (in-sample RMSE 0.0187 vs LOO 0.0533). The ±0.10 band is charged for that.
  • Structural limit: the top published rasters share 0.87–1.00 of their dots, so the corpus is redundant. This file redistributes the recall that already exists in those files; it cannot invent recall they lack.
