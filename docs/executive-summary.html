3. Why 0.2778 is the family ceiling — and what the ceiling is made of

The metric is DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w) with TP_w = Σy∈G maxx p(x)·k(d(x,y)) and a triangular kernel of radius 300 m (3 px) — page 967. Two consequences that the ~90 sibling submissions never exploited:

  • Credit saturates. Because a true pixel is paid once (the max), a dot within 3 px of another dot adds cost but little credit. GEMSDOE32's own forensic table shows the family's progression: 121,131 dots → 0.1922, 60,069 → 0.2477, 44,090 → 0.2600, 37,654 → 0.2778 (kernel credit per dot rising 8.4 → 20.1 px/dot).
  • The scored population is off-catalogue. Known faults are masked out of scoring, so a raster that traces the catalogue beautifully can still score badly. That is why the catalogue holdouts in these repos rank the nine files in the reverse order of the leaderboard (HIDE-RECOVER ρ = −0.849) and why a score-anchored estimator is needed at all.

So the champion is not a better geological idea than its neighbours — it is the same ridge family with the best packing. The remaining 0.17 to the leaderboard lead is surface quality (finding traces the catalogue lacks), which needs the 19-band feature stack and the labels; that is login-walled (IR-SASS3-74).
