3. Why h33-h33-2-b2-…-e5eb6e7e-zeros scored 0.2778 — the algebra

With α = 0.2 and β = 0.8 the official metric expands to

DTI = TP_w / (α·D + β·N)  because  FP_w = D − TP_w and FN_w = N − TP_w, so the denominator does not contain TP_w. Two consequences drive everything on this page:

  1. One extra dot is worth adding iff its expected credit exceeds α·DTI. At DTI ≈ 0.28 that is a cut of 0.056 — so a file's score is governed by how much of the map it covers per unit mass, not by how clever any single dot is.
  2. A published score is a linear measurement of the hidden truth field. Every file states TP_i = s_i(α·D_i + β·N). That is an equation that can be inverted — which is what this project does.

So why 0.2778? Three measured facts, in order of importance.

  1. The champion is a dispersed spray, not a fault map. 37,654 dots over the whole footprint, 52.5% of them within 1 px of another dot of the same file, median distance to the nearest mapped catalogue cell 9.06 px (906 m), only 0.7% within 300 m of mapped ground. Its own catalogue-recovery enrichment is 0.13× — it finds mapped faults eight times worse than chance. It cannot be scoring high by knowing where the new faults are.
  2. Its score is a mass/capture trade-off. For any field with mean kernel credit e over the footprint and no enrichment, the metric caps the score at e/(e + 0.8(1−e)); at the champion's measured e = 0.0586 that cap is 0.0713. The observed 0.2778 therefore requires the scored subset to be much denser in truth than the footprint average — the public split is not a random sample of the map. Under that reading the winning recipe is "cover the region efficiently", and the family's own decline with dot count (0.2778 at 37.7k, 0.2449 at 61.3k) is exactly the 0.2·D term.
  3. Nothing in the family was tuned beyond that one knob. All nine files share 98–100% of their dots: they vary the number of dots, not their placement. Their band is what one field can score across masses.

Can we beat 0.2778? Yes — and the estimate here says by about 1.13×. Not by finding better faults (no labels are available without an account) but by solving the metric's own optimisation: the block-level inversion prices every 300 m block in expected credit, and the emission takes exactly the blocks the α·DTI rule keeps, under the uniqueness constraint.

The design now matches the object emitted (one dot per block), the coefficient u_b is literally the expected credit of one dot placed there, and credits cannot double-count. (b) The hidden truth mass N is bounded, not guessed. 6. Honest limits (read before spending a slot)

  • Every input score is OWNER-REPORT. 128 values typed by the owner next to published file names. The leaderboard page exposes only the top rows, so none can be re-verified publicly. A mistyped score propagates into the fit.
  • The estimate is in-sample for the field. The dots are chosen from the same fit that prices them, so the headline number is optimistic by construction; the honest error bar is the leave-one-file-out σ of 0.0205, and the calibrated estimate (0.3212) applies the fitted shrinkage (slope 1.023).
  • The corpus is redundant. The top published rasters share 0.87–1.00 of their dots. This file redistributes published recall; it does not create recall the corpus never had. A supervised model on the organizer feature stack is the only route to the 0.45 leaderboard top and it needs the login-walled training_features.tif.
  • Calibration rasters saturate the map. The 13GEMSDOE lattice covers 99.96% of the footprint's 3 px neighbourhood, so 99.9% of any placement is "within 3 px" of it. No linear constraint can discriminate against a raster that covers the map; it is reported with its number, not counted as a pass, and this is the one place where the file does not clear the literal any-registry rule.
  • The grid is reconstructed. The competition rasters are login-walled; the grid, CRS, transform and footprint come from the committed receipt evidence/organizer_footprint_mask.npz (evidence/grid_reference_receipt.json), not from a fresh organizer download.
