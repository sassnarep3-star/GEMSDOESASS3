# Decision memo — 2026-10-30

**Question from the standing prompt:** why did `h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros` score 0.2778 (best of the
nine focus sites), can this repo generate a *unique* submission that beats it, and what will that submission score?

**Answer in one line:** 0.2778 is the best point of a single structure-hugging dot family; under this repo's Rule 1
(≤70% of dots within 3 px of any registry raster) the signal that produces it cannot be retained, so the best legal,
unique file here projects to **0.172–0.186**, not above 0.2778. Every number below is a receipt, a measurement or a
labelled projection.

Two facts pin the mechanism:

1. **The family is one dot set.** 99.8% of the champion's dots lie within 3 px of the 13GEMSDOE calibration lattice, and the
   nine files overlap each other at 99.9–100% within 3 px (`evidence/focus_site_candidate_audit_20261028.json`). So 0.2477–0.2778
   is not nine methods; it is one placement scored nine times.
2. **Score falls with dilution.** Over the ≤62k-dot branch, `LB ≈ 0.3222 − 1.265e-6 · dots` fits with residuals ≤ 0.006. The
   champion carries the fewest dots of the family and sits at the top of it: enough coverage of the private truth, not so many
   dots that α·FP accumulates. Adding dots to the same evidence *lowers* the score; removing them, under Rule 1, destroys it
   (below).

The 0.4544 leader (#1 xiaofanhu, ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED, fetched 2026-10-30) is 0.18 above the best GEMSDOE raster. That gap is
off-catalogue signal quality, not dot packing.

## 2 · Can a unique submission beat it? No packing can

**Measured (this session):** the union of all 386 focus-registry rasters' 3-px neighbourhoods covers **5,167,372 of 5,167,373**
scored pixels (99.99998%); exactly **one** pixel is not within 300 m of a published dot
(`evidence/registry_union_coverage_20261030.json`). There is no unclaimed ground to move to, so Rule 1 is a *concentration*
test: a candidate passes only by spreading its dots so that no single raster claims >70% of them.

The measured consequence — three candidate builds, same registry, full coverage (386/386 SHA-verified, 0 gaps):

| Candidate | Dots | Worst single-raster 3-px overlap | hide-and-recover | SGMC-CAL | Projected LB | Rule 1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| LWST 37,654 (2026-10-27) | 37,654 | 99.78% | 0.065702 | — | 0.285 | **FAIL** |
| `…sdr1-spaced-dense21k…` (EXP1) | 21,012 | 62.46% | 0.003412 | 0.039601 | **0.172** | PASS |
| `…literal-lwst-disp21k…` (2026-10-29) | 21,012 | 55.22% | 0.004022 | 0.042851 | **0.186** | PASS |
| `…exp3-sarpu-legal21k…` (EXP3) | 21,012 | **83.25%** | 0.095880 | 0.029683 | 0.129 | **FAIL** |

**Reading it:** spreading dots to satisfy Rule 1 costs roughly a factor of 25 in hide-and-recover and a third in SGMC-CAL.
Nothing in that lane can reach 0.2778. EXP3 is the informative failure: a supervised surface redistributes the signal back onto
the structure and lands champion-level on hide-and-recover (0.0959 vs 0.0992) — but 83.25% of its dots reproduce GEMSDOE32's
`h46-trhv-tworegime-ventconduit` raster, which is precisely what the rule exists to prevent.

An earlier construction error is recorded here so it is not repeated: EXP3's cap was enforced against the *lattice* only
(69.0% inside). The rule is per *any* raster, and the 6,514 lattice-gap dots can still sit within 3 px of a different raster.
Any future builder must allocate the gap set against the union of registry buffers, then re-check the emitted bytes.

## 3 · What a submission will score (calibrated estimator)

SGMC-CAL on the nine focus files versus their leaderboard scores: **Spearman ρ = 0.9620** (p = 3.4e-5, n = 9);
`LB ≈ SGMC × 4.332` reproduces all nine to **±0.0078**
(`evidence/focus9_concordance_20261030.json`). This supersedes the previous note that the holdout "ranks these files in reverse"
(ρ = −0.89): that figure came from the HR-static evaluator applied to a file list containing the known GEMSDOE30/GEMSDOE24
mismatch (IR-SASS3-22). With the correct files and SGMC-CAL the proxy is strongly concordant.

Hide-and-recover is less transferable: legal-lane values are near zero, so its implied projection is ≈0.01. The two proxies
bracket the truth; both put the legal lane far below 0.2778.
