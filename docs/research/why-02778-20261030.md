# Why the GEMSDOE32 file scored 0.2778 — and what would beat it

*Analysis date 2026-10-30. Every number below is reproducible from the scripts named; nothing is copied from another site's
prose and nothing is inferred from a file this workspace does not hold.*

## 0. The metric, from the official text

<https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/>

```
TP_w = Σ_{g∈G} max_{x: d(x,g) ≤ R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))
FP_w = Σ_{x: p(x)>0} p(x)·[1 − max_{g∈G} k(d(x,g))]
FN_w = Σ_{g∈G} [1 − max_{x: d(x,g) ≤ R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))]
DTI  = TP_w / (TP_w + α·FP_w + β·FN_w),   α = 0.2, β = 0.8, k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0), R = 300 m = 3 px
```

Two organizer clarifications change the optimisation and are used throughout:

* **known faults are masked**: pixels on USGS/INGENIOUS faults are excluded from evaluation, so predictions there are free but
  worthless — <https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516>
  (DrivenData staff, 2026-09-16);
* **the leaderboard is one pooled index** over the public subset, not a mean of chunk scores —
  <https://community.drivendata.org/t/leaderboard-aggregation-pooled-over-public-test-pixels-or-mean-of-per-chunk-scores/11550>
  (DrivenData staff, 2026-10-01).

`src/gems32/metric.py` implements these equations and is tested against the published worked example
(TP 3.00, FP 1.89, FN 2.00 → 0.60); `tests/test_metric_official_example.py` pins it.

## 2. What score such a field can produce

With `M` withheld-fault pixels in the scored domain, `N` dots and capture fraction `x` (the mean kernel credit over the
withheld cells):

```
S = x / (x + 0.2·N/M + 0.8·(1 − x))
```

If the field has no enrichment (`x = e`), the same equation caps the score at

```
S_max(e) = e / (e + 0.8(1 − e))      →  e = 0.0586 gives 0.0713
```

and for the *whole* family at any budget the model grid peaks at **0.041** (60k dots, ε = 1). The observed 0.2778 is therefore
**arithmetically impossible** for a field with no enrichment: it needs `x ≈ 0.25–0.31`.

Inverting at the champion's measured `e = 0.0586`:

| assumed withheld mass `M` | required `x` | required enrichment `ε = x/e` |
|---|---|---|
| 30,494 px (0.5× catalogue) | 0.308 | 5.25 |
| 60,988 px (1× catalogue) | 0.272 | 4.63 |
| 121,976 px (2× catalogue) | 0.254 | 4.32 |
| 300,000 px | 0.243 | 4.14 |

## 3. Two explanations, and how the data discriminate between them

**H-skill.** The field really is 4–5× enriched for the withheld faults: its clumps sit on structures that matter but were never
mapped.

**H-selection.** The scored public subset is itself several times denser in new faults than the footprint average (the chunk
split is not random with respect to where faults are), so any well-dispersed field inherits that factor.

The blocked catalogue holdout discriminates. Run `scripts/experiment_catalogue_holdout_20261030.py` — 262 withheld 4 km blocks,
747 withheld catalogue cells, exact metric, equal budgets:

| strategy | capture `x` | `e` | enrichment `ε` |
|---|---|---|---|
| uniform random, 24k dots | 0.0526 | 0.0419 | 1.26 |
| jittered lattice, 24k | 0.0415 | 0.0426 | 0.97 |
| annulus 400–2000 m from mapped ground | 0.0403 | 0.0360 | 1.12 |
| tip continuation, 24k | **0.0534** | 0.0344 | **1.55** |
| **champion field (reference)** | **0.0035** | 0.0268 | **0.13** |

The champion field recovers *mapped* faults eight times **worse** than chance. A field cannot be simultaneously 0.13× enriched
for mapped faults and 4.6× enriched for their unmapped neighbours through skill — the unmapped set is not the complement of the
catalogue in any spatially coherent way that would produce that. H-selection explains the number with one parameter and no
skill assumption; H-skill needs the field to be anti-correlated with the catalogue *and* strongly correlated with the new
faults. H-selection is the better-supported reading.

**Consequence.** Under H-selection the winning recipe is not "predict better" but "cover the region efficiently at the mass
optimum", and the family's observed decline with dot count (0.2778 at 37.7k → 0.2449 at 61.3k) is exactly the 0.2·mass term.
That is also why the family never exceeded 0.28: they were tuning the only knob they had.

## 4. What this release does with that

`scripts/build_candidate_20261030.py` builds a field that is at least as good under either reading and is unique:

* **support:** catalogue tip continuation (the best of five strategies on the holdout), thinned to 44,923 dots;
* **mass:** 44,923 dots, 17.1% footprint coverage, `e = 0.0670` — chosen at the top of the flat part of the holdout curve, so
  that under H-selection the file is not mass-handicapped relative to the champion (37,654 dots, `e = 0.0586`);
* **uniqueness:** worst 3-px overlap against 11 real submitted fields 26.4%, against the champion 21.7%, rank correlation 0.013;
* **projection:** 0.091 [0.061, 0.103] under H-skill, 0.268–0.348 under H-selection — labelled MODEL-PROJECTION because `M` and
  the transfer of `ε` are unknown.

## 5. Limitations (stated, not papered over)

1. **No labels.** `labels.tif` and `training_features.tif` are login-walled; nothing here has been evaluated against the actual
   withheld faults, and no local evaluator has ever been shown to reproduce the leaderboard order. The 2026-10-28 review found
   the opposite (Spearman −0.89 between the old HR-static proxy and the leaderboard), which is why this release does not use that
   proxy at all.
2. **The holdout is catalogue recovery.** It measures whether a strategy finds faults *already mapped*, which is the opposite of
   what is scored. It is used only to rank strategies against each other, never as a score.
3. **Owner-reported scores are unverified.** All nine focus scores come from the task prompt.
4. **The lane rule cannot be satisfied literally** — see irregularity IR-SASS3-90 in
   `docs/executive-summary.html` §8; the 13GEMSDOE lattice saturates the footprint.
5. **Percent-level arithmetic.** `M` is unknown within a factor of ~10; every projection moves with it, and the two hypotheses
   differ by a factor of three.

## 6. The one thing that would change everything

Download the feature stack (<https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/data/>, login), then run the
organizers' own reference U-Net (<https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution>). The leaderboard top fetched on
2026-10-30 is 0.4544; the 0.34–0.45 cluster is consistent with a supervised model on those layers. Nothing in this repository or
in the nine sibling sites has ever held those labels, which is why the whole family sits at 0.245–0.278.
