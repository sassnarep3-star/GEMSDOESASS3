Executive summary — 2026-10-30

Question. Why did h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros score 0.2778 (the best of the nine focus sites), and can this repo generate a unique submission that scores higher?

Answers, in order.

  1. Why 0.2778 (mechanism, measured). The champion family places 38–61k dots directly on structure. Measured on the champion's own bytes, 99.92% of its 37,654 dots lie within 3 px of the 206,895-dot 13GEMSDOE calibration lattice — i.e. the family predicts the same high-probability positions. On the leaderboard, score falls monotonically as those structure-hugging dots are diluted: a fit over the ≤62k-dot branch gives LB ≈ 0.3222 − 1.265e-6 · dots with residuals ≤0.006. The champion is the highest-quality point of that family: dense enough to cover the private truth, not so dense that false positives dominate (α=0.2 still penalises FP). The 0.4544 leader (#1 xiaofanhu, ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED) is roughly 0.18 above every GEMSDOE raster, so the gap is signal quality on off-catalogue faults, not packing.
  2. Can we beat it under this repo's rules? Not with packing. Every packing variant that satisfies Rule 1 loses the positions that carry the signal (see the union-coverage proof below). Legal candidates score ~0.0034–0.0040 hide-and-recover against 0.0992 for the champion: a ~25× gap that no re-packing closed.
  3. What would beat it. New off-catalogue evidence: a supervised surface is capable (EXP3 reached 0.0959 hide-and-recover, i.e. champion-level, at 44% of the champion's dot count) but it converges onto an existing raster's dots and fails Rule 1. The productive direction is new physics/layers that no sibling used, then the legal-cap pack.

3 · Why the 70% any-registry rule caps the achievable score (measured)

Rule 1 (as this repo applies it) forbids more than 70% of a candidate's dots from lying within 3 px of any single registry raster. Measured over all 386 focus-registry rasters, the union of their 3-px neighbourhoods covers 5,167,372 of 5,167,373 scored pixels (99.99998%). Exactly 1 pixel of the scored region is not within 300 m of any published dot.

That makes the rule a concentration test, not a novelty test: since every pixel is near someone's dots, the only way to stay under 70% with any one raster is to spread the dots across many rasters' neighbourhoods. The measured cost of that spread is the whole story of this session: the legal files distribute their dots and score 62.46%/55.22% against their worst single raster, while their recovery collapses to 0.0034–0.0040 hide-and-recover (champion family: 0.0992). A candidate that keeps the signal — dense, structure-hugging dots — lands at 83.25% (EXP3) or 99.78% (the 37,654-dot LWST file) and fails.

So Rule 1, read literally per raster, selects for dispersion and against score. That is the mechanism behind the ceiling described in §5, and the reason §6 asks the owner to decide.

This is a project-protocol finding, not a competition rule. The competition forbids submitting another team's data; it does not forbid predicting the same locations as another team. The trade-off is quantified in §6.

  1. Download the file from the green button at the top of the overview page (or the .zip, which contains the same single GeoTIFF). Verify the SHA-256 shown beside it if you want certainty: sha256sum <file>.

  3. Open the competition's "New submission" page: drivendata.org/competitions/306. Choose File to submit = the .tif (or the .zip). The portal accepts a single-band GeoTIFF that matches the submission format's CRS, shape and geotransform — this file does (EPSG:32611, 3730×3292, 100 m, values in [0,1], NaN outside).
  4. Paste the note (≤140 characters): LWST spaced-density 21k dots; Rule-1 unique (62.5%); canary pass; projected 0.17
  5. Check your weekly allowance first. The official rules allow three submissions per week and one final submission per entity; generative-AI use must be disclosed in the narrative.
  6. Expect 0.172 (PROJECTION), not 0.2778. If that is not acceptable, read §6 before spending the slot.

The portal error seen earlier, "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]", is caused by NaN or out-of-range cells where the portal expects numbers. This file has NaN only outside the competition bounds (which the format explicitly permits) and every finite value is inside [0,1]; the 13-point audit in sdr1_audit.json confirms it.
