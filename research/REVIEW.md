## Why B2 may have performed well—and what we cannot know

The [official metric](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/#performance-metric) computes `TP = Σ_g max_x[p(x)k(d(x,g))]`, `FP = Σ_x p(x)[1-max_g k(d(x,g))]`, `FN = |G|-TP` and `DTI = TP/(TP+0.2FP+0.8FN+ε)`, with `k(d)=max(1-d/300m,0)`. A relatively weak prediction can worsen the ratio if its reduction in weighted false-positive mass outweighs its lost weighted true-positive contribution. Spatial thinning can also reduce redundant mass while retaining coverage; these are hypotheses about a hidden test, not measured causal attributions.

We reproduced the exact local support relations: GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 differ at zero in-footprint pixels; the GEMSDOE31 mirror equals GEMSDOE25 minus the 3,891 dots at distance ≤1 pixel from the mirrored known catalogue; GEMSDOE32 equals GEMSDOE25 minus dots at distance ≤2 pixels, removing a further 2,545 relative to GEMSDOE31. The [staff clarification](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/4) explicitly says the known-fault mask is pixel-exact, not a two-pixel buffer, and newly mapped truth can lie nearby. Thus pruning nearby support risks removing true discoveries. Hidden TP/FP/FN, causality and byte-to-score identity are unavailable; **no higher-score promise or numerical forecast follows**.

## Shared template defects and permissions
